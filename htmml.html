why does SN2 flip the stereocenter but SN1 doesn't always?
SN2 backs the nucleophile in from the opposite side of the leaving group — one clean shove, one inversion. SN1 drops the leaving group first, so the molecule sits flat for a moment before anything attacks it. What does a flat, planar carbon let a nucleophile do that it couldn't do before?

how a session runs

  1. 1

    Bring the problem, not a prompt

    Paste the question, snap the textbook page, or just say what's confusing. No need to phrase it well — that's the part it helps with.

  2. 2

    It answers by asking

    Marginalia leans on questions before it hands you conclusions, so the reasoning ends up in your notes, not just the answer.

  3. 3

    The margin remembers

    Every session adds to a running page per subject — what tripped you up, what clicked, what's worth a five-minute review tomorrow.
